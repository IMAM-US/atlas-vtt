# Forkwater: the Astra representation

This landscape is a working world, not a fixed organization chart. Its archive keeps earlier accounts; its camp supports current work; its central clearing holds the undertaking; its quay holds results. Those meanings are authored interpretations. The river is not an execution pipeline.

Start in **Astra - 01 Forkwater**. Choose the owl at the camp and open the adjacent pin for [[Astra - Current investigator]]. Compare it with the owl at the archive: both are called Astra and share artwork, but their native nodes and conversations differ. Then open the work and result pins.

The **Council perspective** pin opens **Astra - 02 Council**. There, two differently named pieces represent the same current seat. Their common note makes the connection explicit. One piece can be moved, renamed or removed without creating, renaming or retiring a native agent.

The four useful questions are:

- Who or what is represented?
- What are they pursuing, using or responsible for here?
- What happened, and what remains a proposal?
- Which source, time and author support the account?

The actual changes in this example are the original seat's removal, a native conversation fork into the current seat, compaction, a revised proposal, and this separate project assignment. The illustrated geography and board arrangements were authored afterward to make those changes inspectable. See [[Astra - Source ledger]] for exact evidence and limits.

The pins and scene links are native Atlas content. This project does not supply a live OpenRig connector, a send button or a native-agent launcher. [[Astra - Work and native route]] explains the existing native route without pretending a map click executes it.

Both planning results are included as readable presentation copies: [[Astra - Original proposal]] and [[Astra - Revised proposal]]. Queue closure proves delivery of an authored artifact, not human agreement with its recommendation.
