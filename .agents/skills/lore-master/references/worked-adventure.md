# The River's Broken Covenant

An original Lore Master example, expanding the river-spirit situation from the founding conversation. All names and details below are invented. They are example preparation, not facts imported into another campaign or events that have occurred in play.

## DM overview

**Experience:** fantasy discovery, negotiation, and hazardous exploration. Designed as material for one or two sessions; duration is an estimate, not a playtest result. System and party are unspecified, so numerical tuning remains pending.

**Present problem:** a storm is filling the reservoir above Merehaven. An ancient binding in the floodworks is cracking. The wardens offer work securing the failing gate, while ferrymen need help moving lowland families to safety.

**Underlying truth:** Queen Isera once made a seasonal agreement with the river spirit Neris. Her successors closed the floodplain to build permanent farms and converted the covenant into a binding. The floodworks still protect people, but the spirit has been compelled to absorb increasingly dangerous pressure.

**Central question:** can the party obtain safety and freedom together, and whose resources or privileges must change? Repair, release, renegotiation, evacuation, or another coherent approach can resolve different parts of the situation. No ending is privileged.

## Player-facing opening

The festival lanterns are still hanging when the river rises into the market. A ferrywoman poles her boat between the stalls, calling for hands to move sacks and people uphill. From the gatehouse comes a deep metallic knock, repeated three times. A warden announces paid work at the floodworks; the ferrywoman says the lower farms need boats now.

Offer these opportunities without choosing one for the PCs. Optional ties include being a visitor at the festival, knowing a local worker, or seeking passage upriver. A player adopts any personal connection.

## History made visible

The public story says Isera conquered the river. The original covenant allowed spring water into designated lowlands in exchange for protection of the settled ridge. Three present details preserve that history: old houses have upper flood doors; the festival song thanks both queen and river; flood markers predate the fields that now cover the lowlands.

The council's descendants rely on the protected farms for revenue. Farmers rely on those farms for survival. Restoring the floodplain affects both, giving the historical truth immediate consequences without making every resident complicit in the binding.

## Cast and factions

| Person/power | Desire and leverage | Knowledge, limits, and performance cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warden Elian, floodworks authority | Keep homes safe and preserve the council's income; has workers, stores, and gate access | Knows the binding's purpose and the hidden emergency inlet; does not know whether a new pact is possible. Will open access for rescue work; resists permanent land changes. Counts costs aloud. |
| Mara, ferry guild speaker | Evacuate the lower farms; has boats, route knowledge, and neighbors' trust | Knows the seasonal tradition and gallery route; does not know the binding ritual. Will aid anyone moving people. Promised boats cannot also carry the repair crew at the same time. Ties knots while speaking. |
| Tovin, maintenance engineer | Prevent a sudden uncontrolled release; has diagrams, tools, and workers' confidence | Knows gate behavior, structural limits, and the old spillway; suspects supernatural pain but cannot explain the full history. Wants an evacuation signal before opening the outlet. Taps the failing part of a drawing. |
| Neris, bound river spirit | End coercion and recover the seasonal floodplain; can communicate through the gate basin and moderate flow while intact | Knows the covenant and its alteration; cannot freely stop the storm or sustain the damaged binding indefinitely. Can agree to a staged release if water has a safe route. Speaks in remembered water levels. |

Elian hears of covert acts through workers or reports, not automatically. Neris does not know conversations away from connected water. Mara's guild can split its boats only by reducing each task's capacity.

## Revelation table

Each row has three clues reached through different sources. The truth can become clear without reading the private council order.

| Essential conclusion | Independent clues and their observable content | What it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The present binding is failing under accumulated water pressure | Market: flood markers are exceeded while the old channels remain closed. Hall: Tovin's diagram identifies the outlet closure as the pressure bottleneck. Chamber: the gauge rises and the braces crack as more water enters. | Repair, drainage, evacuation, or controlled release rather than searching for an invented saboteur. |

Some clues suggest rather than prove a conclusion alone; together they give robust evidence. Suitable investigation finds the core information. Resolution rules may govern speed, risk, extra detail, or gaining restricted access. A missed check cannot remove every source.

## Obstacles with different choices

**Access at the causeway:** wardens restrict entry because they fear damage to the gate. Elian can authorize repair/rescue access; Tovin can sponsor a technical inspection; the boat landing and ridge path bypass the checkpoint with different exposure. Force can clear the way but may scatter workers needed for repair. The gatekeeper's objective is control of access, not compulsory combat.

**Cracked gallery stairs:** grinding stone, fallen steps, and visible current telegraph a crossing hazard. Ropes and anchors, a temporary bridge, or the longer upper passage can solve it. A setback follows the approach: lost equipment, time, or a stranded worker, resolved under the chosen rules. The upper passage remains physically available.

**Outlet control:** the wheel opens the downstream spillway. Tovin's gauge has a marked safe band. While the reservoir is above that band, abruptly breaking the binding releases water faster than the closed spillway can carry it. Opening the outlet first lowers pressure but sends water toward occupied lowland farms. Evacuation or a new diversion changes that cost. Describe visible water changes before committing to the next step.

**Optional memory puzzle:** a dry alcove contains three movable plaques labeled SPRING, SUMMER, and WINTER; grooves display a reed sprouting, flowering, and sleeping. The inscription says to set the reed's year from waking to rest. Matching spring/sprout, summer/flower, winter/sleep opens a small festival archive with a historic keepsake. Wrong placement leaves it closed and lights the mismatched pair. Inspection can reveal the simple latch; breaking it works but damages the keepsake. No essential clue depends on this puzzle.

## Pressure, outcomes, and rewards

Track three physical conditions: water above the safe band, people still in lowlands, and binding braces intact. If useful, a danger clock can represent brace failure; advance it only for established storm time or actual added damage, with a warning vibration before a new break. PC conversation alone does not advance it.

| Approach achieved | Result and remaining consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Reinforce the binding | Immediate safety buys time, while Neris remains constrained and the underlying land conflict persists. Workers can offer material payment; future release terms remain open. |
| Open the outlet and evacuate | Pressure drops, people reach safety, and some fields flood. The party gains allies and a viable negotiating position; rebuilding and compensation matter. |
| Release Neris abruptly | Freedom is achieved; if pressure remains high and no route is opened, water endangers the lowlands as already signaled. Evacuation or diversion performed earlier reduces that harm. |
| Establish a new covenant | Neris accepts an open seasonal channel and staged release; the council/farmers must actually agree to and implement relocation or engineering work. A promise alone does not move water or houses. |
| Withdraw or pursue another goal | Residents continue their own rescue and repair attempts with their known resources. Resolve unattended changes through storm conditions and their efforts, not automatic punishment for declining the hook. |

Rewards can include payment, passage, recovered records, hospitality, or a freely negotiated river favor. Its exact mechanical benefit requires the campaign's system. Give every party the result their actions actually secured, including partial successes.

## Improvisation checks

If the party kills Elian early, workers, physical controls, public clues, and Neris remain; access and legitimacy change. Do not introduce an identical replacement who restores the expected scene order.

If the party proposes redirecting water into an unused quarry, check geography, capacity, tools, and time. Where these are unspecified, make compatible proposed details explicit; do not declare the quarry impossible solely because it avoids the expected dilemma.

If the spirit is released safely before anyone studies the old pact, honor the solution. Later discoveries can change historical understanding and political accountability without undoing that release.

After actual play, record only resolved events, accepted additions, discovered truths, commitments, and pending decisions. These possible endings are still preparation until played.
