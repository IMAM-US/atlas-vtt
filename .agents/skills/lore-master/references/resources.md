# Resource library

Use this as a source router and annotated reading guide. The codex's organizing principles and examples are original synthesis. Attribution below identifies the techniques borrowed and the limits of the source material reviewed.

Links and accessible descriptions were checked on **2026-10-04**. Availability, editions, prices, and exact rules can change. A checked publisher overview establishes a book's scope; it does not mean the complete book was read. Consult supplied/licensed text before claiming exact chapters, tables, examples, quotations, or detailed procedures. No paid book is required to use the bundled codex.

## Situation, discovery, and space

| Resource | What to draw from it | Application and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A1. Justin Alexander, [Don't Prep Plots](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4147/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots)** | Prepare circumstances and goal-driven opponents while leaving player actions open. | Use for an adventure's causal situation and schemes. A clear shared mission or dramatic outline can still be useful. Accessible primary essay reviewed. |
| **A2. Justin Alexander, [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule)** | Redundant evidence for conclusions essential to progress. | Build revelation tables with at least three clues and independently available sources. Redundancy reduces fragility; it does not guarantee correct interpretation. Accessible primary essay reviewed. |
| **A3. Justin Alexander, [Node-Based Scenario Design](https://thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design.html) and [Inverting the Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/creations/misc/node-design/node-design2.html)** | Connect investigation destinations through leads and multiple options. | Use for people, places, and events that players can investigate in varying order. Inspect actual connectivity rather than applying a slogan about three clues to every node. Accessible primary text reviewed. |
| **A4. Justin Alexander, [Xandering the Dungeon](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/13085/roleplaying-games/xandering-the-dungeon)** | Nonlinear dungeon routes, including the influence of Jennell Jaquays' designs. | Use entrances, loops, and connections when route choice matters. The site's current series title is Xandering the Dungeon; older links use another title. Accessible primary essay reviewed. |

## Select a reading path

- **First playable session:** P1 for preparation, P2 for discoveries, T1 for examples. Start with available articles/videos rather than purchasing everything.
- **Mystery or intrigue:** A1, A2, A3, then D3; establish evidence and information flows before adding twists.
- **World or campaign codex:** W1 and W2 for invention, A1 for situations, D1 for continuing threats; link large-scale material to immediate opportunities.
- **Dungeon or exploration:** A4 for meaningful routes, W2 for inspiration, A2 for essential discoveries.
- **Plot and pacing:** A1, D3, D4; keep outcomes open while giving scenes stakes and emotional variety.
- **Unexpected player direction:** I1 for improvisation reading, A1 for prepared motives/resources, D2 only if progress or danger needs tracking.

## Use knowledge responsibly

Choose the resource that solves the present design difficulty. Do not cite a long bibliography without applying its ideas. When explaining a borrowed technique, attribute it near that explanation; when recommending deeper reading, say what it will help the DM do.

Use reliable primary sources for factual history, culture, geography, folklore, and edition-specific rules when those facts matter. Folklore variants can disagree; specify the tradition/source instead of declaring one universal version. Transform inspiration into original situations without copying an adventure's protected prose, maps, characters, or tables.

If browsing or a book is unavailable, continue with the bundled synthesis and state the limitation only where it affects the request. Never manufacture a quotation, page number, reading receipt, or source-supported claim.
